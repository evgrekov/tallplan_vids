What repeats in all of them:
- 3D character with a skull head (5 different looks), AI male voiceover 185–210 wpm, quiet music bed under non-stop speech.
- One word on screen at a time; "Day N" titles in the 30-day videos.
- Visual changes every ~1.5 s; picture literally shows the spoken word (milk → glass body fills, calcium → tiny workers).
- Generic hashtags (#fyp #educational #skeleton), the hook question often duplicated in the description.
- The channel keeps changing the character — no stable mascot, and no second hit.

What the hit had and the rest do not:
- **Promise about the viewer's own body** ("if you want to increase your height") + result shown in the first second (grows next to a ruler). Everything else is "what happens if…" or third-person comparison.
- **Saveable numbered list** (3 exercises with how-to) → 4.4% saves vs 0.2–0.7% everywhere else.
- **Honest twist** (over 21 it is hard; posture gives 1–2 in) → ~160 of 389 comments post height + age.
- Short: 50 s vs 64–92 s for the rest.
- The same glass character on the same day without the promise and list ("sleep on the floor", also about standing taller) got 6x, not 2000x → the script made the hit, not the character.

Second-best (backrooms, 25x): entertainment, meme stereotypes + trending setting + twist ending. Not our use case, but shows that versus/race with recognizable types beats plain facts on this channel.

Height topic on this channel: "how to look taller" = 2037x; "sleep on the floor → stand taller" = 6x; "tall vs short in the gym" = 3x.
The 10–14 y.o. audience reacts to a personal promise + steps, not to comparisons.

Ideas for TallPlan:
- 🟢 Hook = promise about the viewer's height + visible result in the first second.
- 🟢 Numbered list of 3 actions with how-to (saves), ≤50 s, finish with an honest truth + question that invites "height + age" comments.
- 🟢 Keep: skull/x-ray or glass character, word-by-word captions, ~200 wpm voice, literal visuals, inside-body metaphors.
- 🟡 Day 1→30 format: steady 5–8x, never a breakout.
- 🟡 Versus with meme stereotypes (mogger / gamer) can work as a reach play.
- 🔴 Avoid third-person "tall guy vs short guy" framing for height.

- 🟡 Reverse/sarcastic tips: one-shot reach, do not repeat the same joke.
- 🔴 Separate ad at the end of a long video — every hit hides the app inside the content.

What works:
- **Strange crude characters.** Flat stick figures with pill bodies and emoji faces, one color per family member. The same script as plain text slides got 1.7K vs 371K: the figures are the hook, not the math.
- **Viewer's own numbers → their family on a height chart.** A comment card on top, the kid named (Knox, Spiky). The answer is personal yet everyone checks it against their own family.
- **One simple formula, live.** (dad + mom + 5) ÷ 2 plugged in on screen, big yellow result, range ± 2–4 in. Viewers can repeat it themselves.
- **Comfort / twist ending.** "Not short. Just early." (best ER 4.9%), "+3 above the formula → grandad's tall genes".
- **Comment loop.** CTA "comment your height + parents' heights, you might be next" → 470–1036 comments per hit; about 90% of comments are new stats ("plsss do me") = a free queue of episodes.
- Short: 28–33 s for the hits after the first, AI male voiceover ~140 wpm, no music, static, no cuts.
- Did not help: race-bait hook (14K), correction or humor episode (21K). The plain reply template wins.
- Captions stuffed with long AI-written SEO paragraphs (unknown effect).

Ideas for TallPlan:
- 🟢 "Reply to a comment" series: viewer's stats → stick-style family on a chart → TallPlan prediction → range + comfort line → CTA for the next comment. Fully 2D, cheap, no actor.
- 🟢 Our own "weird" consistent character set (one color per family member) instead of realistic people.
- 🟡 Use TallPlan's real prediction instead of the textbook formula: proof of the app, but the formula's repeatability is part of the appeal.

Ideas for TallPlan:

- 🟡 Plain worksheet look, no cute decoration; the physical prop (foam hand + paper) can be faked in 2D, but the real-prop look is part of the charm.

## Format: UGC reaction + app demo (X @cesaralvarezll, 2026-11-24)

Source: https://x.com/cesaralvarezll/status/2124948534596403455 — a marketer's post selling AI UGC clones: original UGC video (claimed 8M+ views, creator and app not named) side by side with its AI clone. Not a TikTok channel, so no sheet rows. Video: Drive `02_sources/references/x/cesaralvarezll/`.

Structure (~11 s, no voice, quiet sound):
1. 0–3.5 s **reaction hook:** a real girl on a bed, hand on forehead then over mouth, shocked; caption "4 years at the gym and I just NOW find this?!?!".
2. 3.5–11 s **app demo:** the app is filmed with a phone pointing at another phone screen (fingers, blanket in the background), not a clean screen recording; finger scrolls the training plan; caption "An app that plans and tracks all your workouts!!".

What works:
- Relatable regret in the hook ("4 years and only NOW") — the viewer feels they are late too; the answer is the app.
- The demo looks amateur (filmed screen, real hand), so it reads as a friend's tip, not an ad.
- Very short: one reaction + one demo shot, the product is on screen from 3.5 s.
- In the AI clone only the reaction person is generated; the app footage is reused as is. The face is the only expensive part.

Ideas for TallPlan:
- 🟢 Reaction hook (AI-generated 19–20 y person, shocked) + filmed-screen demo of TallPlan's prediction / routine; caption like "17 and I only NOW find out how tall I'll be?!".
- 🟡 Pair it with @todd.f.moir's hidden-answer idea: the demo reveals the predicted height last.
